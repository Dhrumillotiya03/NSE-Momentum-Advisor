# PRE-REGISTRATION — distance × volatility-regime interaction in the P(touch) table

**Written 2026-08-31, BEFORE running any test. Frozen. Amendments appended
with reason, never silently edited.**

**STATUS: NOT YET RUN — deliberately blocked on data.** See "When to run".

## Where this came from

The August 2026 month-end review (first month logged entirely under the
P(touch) table AND under min-separation). Headline: S1 32.1% / R1 34.7% at
the production horizon, against logged probabilities averaging ~44% — a
weighted mean calibration gap of **−10.1pp**, with 7 of 10 probability bins
overconfident.

That gap is **mostly not a model defect** and this pre-registration is not an
attempt to close it. August realised 5.4% annualised volatility on the index:
the **0.3rd percentile** of every 16-session window since 2010, against a
13.3% trailing figure. `reach_probability_v2` keys on distance × *trailing
252-day* volatility, so it cannot see a regime that shifts inside the month.
Conditioned on the volatility that actually materialised, the model was
calibrated where vol came in at or above assumed (**+5.1pp**, mildly
underconfident, n=244) and hot where it came in quieter (**−11.7pp**,
n=1431). Discrimination was strong throughout: AUC 0.839, Brier skill +26.6%
over the base rate, corr(predicted, actual) across the 16 horizon lengths
**+0.997**.

Two candidate explanations were generated by that review and **both were
tested and rejected there**, so neither is in scope here:

* *Sub-5-day horizons use sqrt-extrapolation and are broken.* Native tables
  exist only at 5/10/15/21d and `_interpolate_horizon_prob` requires
  bracketing, so 1–4 day horizons fall through to a sqrt rescale of the 21d
  table. Raw gap on that path was −13.7pp vs −9.9pp on native tables — but
  conditioned on volatility the paths are indistinguishable (quiet subset:
  interpolated −11.2, native −12.9, extrapolated −12.3). The apparent penalty
  was composition: the sub-5-day rows are the month's last four sessions,
  which were also its quietest.
* *A shorter trailing volatility window would have seen it coming.* Worth
  asking, because the 16-variant model sweep varied the vol ESTIMATOR
  (Parkinson, Garman-Klass, Rogers-Satchell, close-gap) but every one of them
  uses `.tail(252)` — window length has never been tested. It does not help.
  Every window overestimated August by 30–41% (median realised/assumed
  0.59–0.70 at 21/42/63/126/252d). The best (42d) improves mean absolute
  error only 12.0pp → 11.0pp while its correlation with realised vol collapses
  0.406 → 0.258.

## The hypothesis that survived

**H1. The table's distance sensitivity is too steep relative to its volatility
sensitivity — i.e. the distance × vol INTERACTION is under-modelled.**

Near levels are volatility-insensitive; far levels are not. Splitting August's
calibration gap by distance *within* each volatility regime:

| \|distance\| | normal/busy vol | n | quiet vol | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3% | −3.5 pp | 61 | −4.0 pp | 316 |
| 3–5% | +3.8 pp | 78 | −11.9 pp | 469 |
| 5–8% | +8.3 pp | 64 | −20.1 pp | 388 |
| >8% | +15.2 pp | 41 | −8.3 pp | 258 |

The 0–3% band misses by ~4pp in *both* regimes. Beyond that the gap flips
sign with the regime and grows with distance. Mechanism: a distant level needs
a large move, so it is exactly where the coarse trailing-vol bucket hides the
most variation; a near level gets touched by noise in any regime.

**This does not contradict** the 2026-08-15 walk-forward finding that the
table is well calibrated in distance (74 dates, 0/6 buckets miscalibrated,
max error ±1.3pp). That test pooled across volatility regimes; this one
conditions on them. A model can be unconditionally calibrated and still
conditionally wrong in offsetting directions — which is what the sign flip
above literally is.

## Why this is NOT being acted on now

One month. An extreme one — the 0.3rd percentile of sixteen years. The
normal-vol cells hold 41–78 observations across 14 dates, and the panel's
statistical unit is the DATE, not the row (61 symbols on one session share
the market's move). Every prior attempt in this repo to adjust the S/R model
off a promising in-sample pattern has failed out of sample, and the ceiling
analysis says the (distance × vol) bucket table already extracts ~99% of what
those two axes can express — an oracle table fitted **on the holdout itself**
beats production by only +0.008 OOS correlation.

So the prior here is genuinely low, and this pre-registration exists mainly to
**fix the decision rule before the data arrives**, so a pattern that appears
in three months' logs cannot be rationalised into an adoption.

## When to run

Not before **six** complete months logged under both the P(touch) table and
min-separation — i.e. not before **2027-02**, and only if the normal-vol
subset holds ≥400 observations across ≥40 distinct dates. August 2026 is
month one. Running it earlier is explicitly out of order.

Rationale for six: the effect is defined by contrast BETWEEN volatility
regimes, so the sample must contain both. One quiet month gives one side of
the comparison and 244 normal-vol observations from its own edges — not
enough to fit an interaction on.

## What will be tested

A single amendment to the touch table's construction: **a finer or
interaction-aware volatility axis**, evaluated exactly as
`sr_build_touchtable.py` tables already are — out-of-sample correlation
between predicted and realised touch rate on a held-out period, at 5/10/15/21d.

Candidate configs, fixed now (no others may be added later):

1. **V1** — vol axis split from 3 buckets to 5, edges at the empirical
   quintiles of trailing-252d vol.
2. **V2** — distance edges made vol-relative: express distance in units of
   the name's own 21-day sigma rather than in percent.
3. **V3** — V2 restricted to levels beyond 3% (near levels keep percent
   edges, since they are measurably vol-insensitive above).

V2 is the direct expression of the mechanism: if the gap is that distance
means different things at different volatilities, then measuring distance in
sigma removes the interaction by construction rather than by adding cells.

## Decision rule (fixed before seeing results)

Adopt a config only if **all four** hold:

1. Out-of-sample correlation improves by **≥ +0.02** over the production
   table (the same bar the 16-variant sweep and the Parkinson estimator study
   used — no new, easier threshold).
2. It wins at a **majority of the four horizons** (5/10/15/21d).
3. A paired block bootstrap over dates gives a 95% CI on the correlation
   delta that **excludes zero**.
4. **P(touch) stays monotone non-decreasing in horizon** for every cell
   (verified 1–23d). This invariant broke once already via a nearest-table
   match and is non-negotiable regardless of accuracy.

If ≥2 configs clear, adopt the one with the **fewest cells**, not the highest
score — thin cells are what the fallback machinery exists to paper over, and
MIN_CELL is already measured inert at current data volume.

If 0 configs clear: the interaction is declared noise, this line of work
CLOSES, and the August pattern is recorded as what a 0.3rd-percentile month
does to an unconditional table. Per the standing rule from the S/R model
sweep, a near miss is a rejection and is **not** grounds to try a fourth
config.

## What must NOT be done in the meantime

* **No flat recalibration.** A −10.1pp shift lifts Brier skill from +26.6% to
  +31.2% *on August* and would be straightforwardly wrong in the next normal
  month. The gap is regime-dependent, not constant.
* **No shortening of the trailing vol window** — tested and rejected above.
* **No conditioning on realised volatility in production.** Realised vol over
  the horizon is not knowable at log time. It is a diagnostic for explaining a
  month after the fact, never an input to a live probability.
